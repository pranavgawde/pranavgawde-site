Executive Summary
KJ Somaiya Polytechnic · May 2026
▼

"KJ Somaiya Polytechnic has the intent and ecosystem to build something genuinely useful. What it currently lacks is a technically grounded plan. This document is that plan."

Finding 01

The current approach is hardware-first, understanding-last. Buying robot arms before students can explain a control loop is the most common and most expensive mistake in institutional robotics.

ApproachThis plan reverses the order deliberately. Software proficiency and conceptual understanding come before any physical equipment is touched.
Finding 02

Start before the lab is built. A 4-week Bootstrap course using ₹2.5L of DIY kits can run in any classroom. Students arrive at the lab already knowing what a sensor feels like.

ActionOrder 12 DIY kits and start within 30 days. View the Bootstrap course plan →
Finding 03

2000 sq ft is workable if zoned deliberately. Six defined stations support 30–40 concurrent students. The layout is specified station by station.

See itEvery zone is specified station by station, with equipment, student capacity, and sq ft. View the 2000 sq ft draft floor plan →
Finding 04

Phase 1 Foundation Lab costs ~₹11L. Most of the learning stack is open-source software on commodity hardware.

FundingBegin without waiting on CSR. Make the ask at Month 3, once a live TurtleBot SLAM demo is running — not Month 0. Mphasis is the right first target. View full budget →
The Plan at a Glance
Three phases · independently fundable · ₹2.5L to ₹1Cr+
▼
Phase 0 · ₹2.5–3L
Bootstrap — Start before the lab exists
4-week course · 12 DIY kits · any classroom
▼

A classroom course using robotics DIY kits. No lab construction needed. Students assemble, program, and compete with physical robots in 4 weeks. By the time Phase 1 lab opens, they already understand sensors, actuators, and control logic.

Who: Any student, any background. School students, polytechnic freshers, faculty.
What: 12 DIY 4-in-1 robotics kits. Sensor packs. Shared laptops.
Outcome: Students can name every robot component, write a basic control loop, and have built and raced a physical robot.
Phase 1 · ₹11–13L
Foundation Lab — 2000 sq ft, software-first
Months 1–6 · 30–40 students · 6 stations
▼

Six zoned stations covering simulation, electronics, mobile robotics, computer vision, fabrication (PCB milling + laser cutting), and discussion. Everything runs on open-source software.

Anchor equipment: 10 Ubuntu laptops, 3 TurtleBot 3, RPLidar A1, Bambu Lab A1 Mini, xTool D1 Pro 20W laser, CNC PCB mill, Arduino + ESP32 + XIAO kits, Intel RealSense D435i.
Unlocks: Foundation 12-week course (Track 1).
CSR pitch window: Month 3, once lab is live and demoable.
Phase 2 · ₹29–35L
Applied Lab — expand to 4000 sq ft, add arms and AI
Months 6–18 · 60+ students · CSR funded
▼

Robot arms, GPU workstation, Jetson AGX Orin, PLC automation rig, advanced fabrication. A myCobot doing live pick-and-place is a 10-minute CSR story.

Anchor equipment: 2× myCobot 280 Pi, RTX 4070 GPU workstation, 2× Jetson AGX Orin, Siemens S7-1200 PLC, TurtleBot 4, Bantam Tools PCB mill, xTool S1 laser upgrade.
Unlocks: Applied Systems 16-week course (Track 2).
Phase 3 · ₹60–90L
Industry Lab — 6000 sq ft, cobot, drone, research
Year 2+ · Industry-grade · Startup launchpad
▼

Universal Robots UR5e cobot, drone program, research-grade measurement. The lab becomes a reference for polytechnic robotics in Maharashtra. Projects enter riidl incubation and national competitions.

Critical note: The most important Phase 3 investment is a full-time lab engineer at ₹5–6L/yr. Hardware without stewardship becomes a graveyard within 18 months.
India-focus: Projects should target Indian problems — agri-robotics, assistive tech, SME automation.

Lab Dependency — Track Sequencing

Track 0 needs only a classroom and 12 DIY kits. Track 1 requires Phase 1 lab. Track 2 requires Phase 2 lab. Each course track is deliberately unlocked by the corresponding lab phase.

Track 0
Bootstrap — 4 Weeks
No lab needed · DIY kits only · any classroom
▼
Who This Is For

Anyone — before the lab exists. School students (Grade 9–12), polytechnic freshers, faculty, curious adults. No prior knowledge assumed.
